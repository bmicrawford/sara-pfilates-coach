Hi {{first_name | default: 'there'}}, you told us leakage with physical activity (coughing, sneezing, lifting, exercise) is your main concern. Here's how to aim your practice at it:
- Make the pulse sharp. The short, forceful, open-mouth "HA" engages your transversus abdominis at the exact moment of the pulse, alongside your voluntary pelvic floor squeeze. Timing is the skill you're training.
- Pick your highest-scoring movements, the ones where you felt your pelvic floor work hardest. If you're unsure: Lunge, Bridge, Hovering, Cat Cow or Butterfly. Pregnant and past 12 weeks? Avoid any PfilAtes™ movements that involve lying on your back (Butterfly, Bridge, Corkscrew), and check with your OB or midwife.
- Pre-squeeze in real life. Try a quick voluntary pelvic floor squeeze just before you cough, sneeze, laugh or lift.
- You're already ahead. Only 29% of women with urinary leakage see a clinician about it, about 3 in 10 (Hou 2025, a review of 80 studies). Doing something about it matters. It's also worth talking with your own doctor.

(This figure is for urinary incontinence only, from the study abstract, and the studies varied a lot.)
