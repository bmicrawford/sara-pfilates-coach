## 1. Origin: the 2008–2009 movement screen (SUFU11, TGR)
- SUFU11 methods: "Between September 2008 and September 2009 real time surface EMG data was collected from the pelvic floor of 6 multiparous women ages (40–47) during performance of 100 different mat Pilates, Yoga, and personal training movements." Each movement was videotaped and synchronized to EMG to find the point of peak pelvic floor engagement. Movements were rated on "multiples of baseline resting EMG".
- SUFU11 results, mean multiples of baseline (resting) EMG, with the point of peak engagement: Side laying straight leg circles 21.6 (forward sweep 45° above horizontal); Side laying bent knee lifts 26.1 (top of leg lift); Bridging 26.1 (top of bridge, gluteals engaged); Corkscrew 17 (hips flexed, legs over body); Lunges 41.4 (deepest point in the lunge); Squats 18 (deepest point in the squat); Cat into cow 15.7 (full cat pose); Butterfly 24.9 (knees together, feet pressed together); All Fours Side Leg Lift 21.3 (top of the lift); Hovering 28 (top of the movement during pelvic curl).
- SUFU11 conclusion: "Certain plyometric movements induce effective co-recruitment of the pelvic floor musculature. These 10 movements are considered to be the most effective pelvic floor engagers from the large number of movements studied."
- TGR: "In 2008, we began a series of experiments… Six multiparous women aged 40 to 47 years performed 100 mat Pilates, Yoga, and personal training movements using the VESy system." Ten were selected because they were "Most suitable for the public at large", "Provided an array of postures", and "Represented templates for functional movement."
- **CONFLICT (number of movements):** 100 movements [SUFU11; TGR] vs "One hundred and twenty" [SEN p.4] vs "over 120" [MAG; SITE].
- **CONFLICT (start year):** experiments began in 2008 [TGR; SEN] vs "In 2009 we created a program of functional movement… called Pfilates." [MAG]. These may be different milestones.
- **Withdrawn (Dr C, Oct 25, 2026):** [SEN p.3] says PfilAtes increases pelvic floor activity "by up to 10x a standard Kegel." SUFU11 multiples are against resting baseline, not a Kegel. The Kegel comparisons (SGS16) are +21% to +56%. On Oct 23, 2026 Dr C had confirmed that Sara could say PfilAtes™ movements can activate the pelvic floor up to 10 times more than a standard Kegel, and that she could say "10x" only as "up to 10x a standard Kegel." That claim is withdrawn. Sara does not say 10x, ten times, or 10 times more than a Kegel. A Kegel comparison uses the SGS16 figure: in a study of 100 consecutive patients, each of the 10 movements averaged 21% to 56% more pelvic floor activity than the person's own Kegel, worded as "suggests", never "proven".

## 2. VESy method
- VESy = "Video, EMG, Synchronization" [TGR], a "modification of gait analysis for evaluation of the pelvic floor" [TGR]. Surface EMG channels: pelvic floor, gluteals, abdominals, lower extremity adductors [TGR Figure; MP 77; VC]. MyoResearch XP software [MP 80]. SUFU12 used Noraxon wireless sensors.
- **Note:** the request described VESy as "ultrasound movement analysis". No source supports that. All sources describe surface EMG plus video.
- Pulse phase [TGR]: "While making a small range of motion around the point of peak passive pelvic floor engagement, the subject adds a series of rapid maximal voluntary pelvic floor contractions in coordination with proper use of breath." Example: pelvic floor EMG "from 10 mV during the isolated Kegel to 30 mV during the pulse." (**Unit note:** mV as printed. MP 78 reports bridging Channel A as "20.0 µV".)
- [SEN p.7] "The Power is in the Pulse!": 1. keep the movement small; 2. percussive breathing; 3. voluntary pelvic floor contraction.
- Personalization: "By identifying each subject's best 2 or 3 movements, the subject is able to concentrate his or her fitness efforts more efficiently." [TGR] **CONFLICT:** MAG suggests "3 or 4 movements" for "5 minutes per day". The current program is 10 minutes a day, 5 morning + 5 evening [SITE; PHX]. [SEN]/[PW] say to pick the top 1–3 by sensation (0–3 scale) and, with prolapse, to omit any movement that causes pain.
- SUFU12 (single subject, 55-year-old woman, jump rope): "Skipping rope (MOB = 10.1) and running rope (MOB = 9.5) were found to induce a greater degree of pelvic floor muscular activity than jumping rope (MOB = 3.9)… 61% and 59% greater average mean amplitude than jumping rope." Baseline was 9.7 uV. **CONFLICT:** the abstract's own table lists Running 3.9 and Jumping 9.5, swapped relative to the text. One subject only.

## 3. VESy EMG study, Kegels vs specialized movements (SGS16)
- "Between January 2014 and May 2015 one hundred consecutive patients referred for pelvic floor rehabilitation" did 10 movements wearing wireless surface EMG (pelvic floor, gluteals, lower abdominals, adductors), with video synchronized to 4-channel EMG. Each clip began with an isolated pelvic floor contraction. Mean peak PF activity in the isolated contraction was compared with peak PF activity during the movement.
- Results, "Percent Greater Than Isolated Kegel" [SGS16], all p<0.001 ("p,0.001" printed for Hovering):

## 4. Clinical outcomes
### 4a. 4-week VESy/Pfilates chart review, the source of 74% and 33% (SGS14)
- Objective: clinical response to "a 4 week program of pelvic floor rehabilitation using the Pfilates Method in combination with Video-EMG Synchronization (VESy)."
- Methods: "Retrospective chart review of patients… through the Center for Pelvic Floor Medicine between 2012 and 2013."
- Sample: "60 charts were selected for review. Twenty-five patients completed pre and post pelvic floor manometry. Eighteen patients completed pre and post bladder symptom questioners (UDI-6). Twelve patients provided an estimate of global improvement".
- Results: "The mean improvement in pelvic floor strength, as estimated using vaginal balloon manometry, was 33% (13.2 – 16.9 cm H2O, p=.006). The mean improvement in UDI-6 score was 33% (9.3 – 6.3, p=.001). Among patients with a score greater than 0 on pre UDI-6 the average improvement in overall bladder symptoms was 74% 4 weeks after completion of the program."
- Conclusion: completing the program "is associated with short term improvement in pelvic floor strength and bladder symptoms."
- **Arithmetic flag (not changed):** the printed means, 13.2 → 16.9 cm H2O, work out to about a 28% rise, not 33%. The 33% may be a mean of per-patient changes. The source does not say.

### 4b. TGR (2017), the published version
- "Clinical data from our laboratory indicate that over the course of a 6- to 8-week training period, subjects achieved statistically significant improvement in the following: 1. Pelvic floor strength as measured with balloon manometry, +33% (P = .006) 2. Subjective symptoms; 74% improvement in bladder symptoms (urgency, frequency, incontinence)". The citation is ref 4, the SGS14 poster. TGR gives no sample size.
- TGR also says the Cochran(e) Review supports pelvic floor muscle exercise as first-line conservative therapy for women with stress, urge, and mixed incontinence. It recommends this training be accessible "via community fitness professionals and or physical therapists."
- **CONFLICT (timeframe):** "4 week program", with the 74% measured "4 weeks after completion" [SGS14]; "After 4-Weeks" [SEN p.5]; "6- to 8-week training period" [TGR; STRAT].
- Other places 74% appears: [SITE] "participants reporting a 74% improvement in bladder symptoms"; [PHX] "In our work, participants have seen a 74% improvement in bladder symptoms."
- **Do not quote 74% for community women (Dr C, Oct 23, 2026):** The 74% bladder-symptom figure in these sources must not be quoted as a result for community-dwelling women or as a general PfilAtes™ result. Sara may cite 74% only as the 2023 Evergreen Commons result for seniors. The 33% strength figure may still be cited from this chart review, with no timeframe, and it must not be paired with 74% as a community-women result.

### 4c. "100 Subject Cohort Clinical Outcomes study (2015–2016)" (MP 96–99 only; no primary document found)
- Measures: global assessment (% improvement), pelvic floor strength (vaginal manometry), voiding frequency, UDI-6, IIQ-7, incontinent episodes per 24 h.
- As written: "50% of patients showed global improvement"; "pelvic floor strength increased by 62%"; "Voiding frequency was reduced by 20%"; UDI-6 "decreased by 43%"; IIQ-7 "reduced by 63%"; "incontinent episodes per 24 hours were reduced by 64%."
- **CONFLICT:** strength +62% here vs +33% [SGS14/TGR], and UDI-6 −43% here vs 33% [SGS14]. This is a different cohort, and only the study guide reports it.

### 4d. 28-day result (approved by Dr C, Oct 23, 2026)
- Historical site wording, kept here as written. Men's page: "80% of our participants are at least 50% improved by day 28" and "average 50% improvement". Women's copy: "Pilot study in senior women: 80% reported a 50% improvement in bladder and bowel symptoms in 28 days". [SITE]
- **CONFLICT:** the wording differs, and no study document backs either. [STRAT] had excluded them from approved claims.
- **Approved wording:** 80% of people who use the PfilAtes™ program (or at least 50%) improved in 28 days of training. Sara uses this sentence. She does not add other 28-day numbers.

## 5. Independent RCT: Au et al., PM&R 2020 (AU)
- Au D, Matthew AG, Alibhai SMH, et al. *PM R* 12 (2020) 55–63, doi:10.1002/pmrj.12157, NCT02233608. Toronto, Canada; Movember Discovery Grant. The CV says: "Independent… Dr. Crawford was not an author of this study."
- Men having radical prostatectomy, ages 40–80. 226 assessed, 122 eligible, 50 randomized (25 per arm), 37 completed (target was 70). Recruitment ran Aug 2015–Mar 2017.
- Control: isolated pelvic floor muscle exercise (PFMX, 30/day in weeks 1–2, up to 180/day in weeks 7–26). Intervention: PFMX plus Pfilates (shown: butterfly, bridge, lunge) and Hypopressives (APFX), at comparable volume.
- Feasibility: recruitment 41%; adherence PFMX 91.1% vs APFX 82.7% (P = .03); attrition 26%; "no trial-related adverse events."
- 26-week bladder diary, favoring APFX: total 24-h leakage events 2.07 vs 0.94 (rate ratio 0.45); waking-hours leaks 2.37 vs 1.02 (rate ratio 0.43, 95% CI 0.20–0.91). **CONFLICT (CI typo in paper):** the 0.45 CI is printed as "0.2-0.98" (abstract), "0.21-0.98" (text), and "0.22, 0.98" (Table 4).
- No between-group difference in pad-test urine loss, HRQOL totals, IPSS, or IIEF-5. Continent at 12 weeks: 21% (APFX) vs 15% (PFMX).
- Conclusion: "feasible… preliminary data suggest a potential benefit in aiding recovery of urinary control. Larger studies with longer follow-up are warranted." Level II. The assessor was not blinded; follow-up was 26 weeks.
- **Wording:** the paper tested Pfilates **plus Hypopressives**. Growth drafts call this "PfilAtes-type core exercises".

## 6. Other official method content (MP, SEN)
- The pelvic floor works with the gluteals, abdominals, and inner thighs [SEN p.6]. Four integrated groups: pelvic floor, adductors, abdominals, posterior hip muscles [MP 17].
- Type I and Type II fibers [MP 75]. "Pelvic floor health is a fitness issue"; disorders are "principally neuromuscular" [cert exam Q7]; results depend on "ongoing attention to pelvic floor conditioning" [Q6].
- The 28-day program: pre and post 3-day bladder diaries plus a C10 questionnaire, with the post diary on Days 26–28 [SEN pp.10–19].
- MAG: "over 500" certified Pfilates instructors in North America (undated).
- CV active research, no results yet: plyometrics and pelvic floor; efficacy of VESy; Patterned Electro-Neuromuscular Stimulation for OAB.
- Origin story [STRAT; speaker sheet A `86e4a873….png`]: after fistula repairs in Niger (2004–2008), about half the women stayed incontinent. Related paper: Ascher-Walsh CJ, …, Crawford B, … *Int Urogynecol J* 2010;21(11):1385-1390 [CV].
